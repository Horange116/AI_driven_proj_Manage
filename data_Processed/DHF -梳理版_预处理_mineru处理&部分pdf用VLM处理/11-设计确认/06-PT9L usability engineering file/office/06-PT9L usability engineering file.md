The purpose of Usability Engineering, also known as Human Factors Engineering, for the Infrared Forehead Thermometer is to provide a systematic process to analyze, specify, develop, and evaluate the usability of the device in relation to safety. By considering the specific needs and abilities of users, Usability Engineering enhances the usability, safety, and user experience of the Infrared Forehead Thermometer, reducing the likelihood of errors and optimizing the overall effectiveness.

## 1.4. **Tailoring of the usability engineering effort** 

Tailoring the usability engineering effort for a Infrared Forehead Thermometer is essential to address the specific risks and complexities associated with its use. The level of effort required for usability engineering can vary significantly depending on the nature of the device, its functions, and potential risks. Several considerations should guide the tailoring process:

**Risk Assessment and Severity:** 

Begin by assessing the potential risks associated with the Infrared Forehead Thermometer's usability. Focus on identifying hazards and their potential causes that could lead to harm. Consider the severity of potential harm when determining the scope of usability engineering efforts. High-severity risks should receive greater attention.

**Device Complexity:** 

Consider the complexity of the Infrared Forehead Thermometer's functions and user interactions. Devices with more intricate operations may require more extensive usability engineering efforts. The complexity could range from simple devices like lancing devices to more complex ones like haemodialysis equipment.

**Novelty:** 

For novel devices lacking post-production data, prioritize the severity of potential harm over estimating occurrence probability. This approach helps tailor usability engineering efforts to address worst-case scenarios and focuses on minimizing harm.

**Modification and Change:** 

When modifying an existing user interface, focus on the changed elements and their effects on device use. If unchanged elements remain unaffected, additional usability engineering effort might not be needed. Changes that do not impact the user interface or use specifications might not necessitate additional effort.

**Previous Records:** 

If previous usability engineering records are available for an existing user interface, leverage these records to streamline the usability engineering effort. Tailor activities based on the changes made to the user interface and the relevance of previous records.

**User Research and Testing:** 

Tailor the extent of user research and testing based on device complexity and risks. Research efforts might involve a smaller or larger number of participants. The usability testing scope could vary from involving a comprehensive set of users to specific user groups with distinct responsibilities.

**Summative Evaluation:** 

Tailor the quantity and complexity of summative evaluations based on the risk assessment. For some cases, a single usability test with a single user group might suffice to assess hazard-related use scenarios. In other instances, multiple sessions with different user groups might be necessary to assess various scenarios.

**Iterative Process:** 

Good usability engineering practice involves iterative evaluations. If initial summative evaluations indicate modifications are needed, focus subsequent evaluations on those specific elements of the user interface affected by the changes.

**Usability Engineering Plan:** 

Develop a tailored usability engineering plan that outlines the specific activities, participants, and methods for each phase of usability engineering. This plan ensures that efforts are aligned with the device's characteristics and risks.

By tailoring the usability engineering effort to the unique characteristics of the Infrared Forehead Thermometer, you can effectively identify, mitigate, and control usability-related risks, ensuring the safety and effectiveness of the device's user interface.

# 9. **Summative evaluation of the usability of the user interface**

The purpose of a summative evaluation is to assess the usability of the Infrared Forehead Thermometer device’s user interface for successful task completion related to hazard-related use scenarios.

Through the qualitative investigations from usability tests, the 30 representative operators have gone through 3 times of tests each, and among the 90 tests in total. And during all the test,

(1) the use error happened was within the ±95% scope, so we believe, the device is convenient to use.

(2) For the maintenance, most of them know how to maintain the device, only 3 of them have to check the user guide to conform if the device can be soaked or immersed to clean. This risk can be avoid by users to check the user guide again.

(3) For the operation environment, several user have to check the user manual to conform where to use, this risk can be avoid during use. Only 3 user think the device can be disturbed by wireless equipment such as cell phone.

(4) For the important indications, all users can clarify which condition the device can not be used.

As the results above, we believe, the IFT Infrared Forehead Thermometer can be used properly and easily with the user manual provided to users

No new hazards, hazardous situations, or risk scenarios are discovered, improvements are not necessary.
